# Candidate Rights Baseline

Nora is designed so candidate protections are executable product behavior, not only policy text.

## Candidate-visible guarantees

1. **AI disclosure** — the session requires explicit consent to an AI-led interview.
2. **Transcript disclosure** — transcript processing requires separate explicit consent.
3. **Correction** — candidate transcript turns can be corrected while preserving the original.
4. **Appeal** — candidates can request review and reference the exact turns in dispute.
5. **Integrity flags are not verdicts** — all integrity signals require human review.
6. **Evidence provenance** — competency evidence references the source turn that supports it.
7. **No hidden sensitive-trait score** — face, attractiveness, accent prestige, inferred emotion, religion, ethnicity, gender, health, and similar traits are outside the evaluation schema.

## Why correction is constrained

Candidates may correct transcription errors. They cannot edit interviewer questions, silently delete the original answer, or rewrite audit history.

The revision record is separate from the current transcript so reviewers can see what changed.

## Why integrity is progressive

A normal screening interview should not automatically inherit the surveillance burden of a high-stakes certification exam.

The session therefore declares its integrity level explicitly. Implementations should activate only the controls justified by that level and applicable law/policy.

## Human review boundary

Nora can surface:

- insufficient evidence;
- contradictions;
- evaluator disagreement;
- integrity signals;
- candidate appeals.

These are review inputs. They are not proof of misconduct and are not autonomous final hiring decisions.
